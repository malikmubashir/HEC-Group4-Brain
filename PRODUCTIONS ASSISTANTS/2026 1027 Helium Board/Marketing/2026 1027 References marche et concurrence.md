# Helium: market research brief (France, premium 100% AI personal butler)

Prepared 27 October 2026. All URLs retrieved 27 October 2026. Each figure carries: source [n], data date, reliability class (OFFICIAL = official statistics; COMPANY = company page or help centre; PRESS = press; AGGREGATOR = third-party aggregator or analyst; VENDOR = vendor marketing or sales content), and a status tag: FACT (sourced as stated), ESTIMATE (our derivation, calculation shown) or UNKNOWN (not found; measurement route suggested).

Working price hypotheses under test: Personal 29 €, Premium 79 €, Family 129 € TTC per month. No payments in the MVP; every third-party-visible action requires user confirmation.

## 4. Market gaps and pain points (synthesis)

1. **Agents are not sold in France.** Every proactive consumer agent is EEA-excluded in October 2026 (dots on Pro, Gemini Spark, Chrome auto browse, Claude Duet, Siri AI, Muse) [31], [38], [39], [43], [47], [52]. The French cadre who wants delegation is left with chat plus approval-gated connectors. This is a timing window, not a permanent moat; Business Premium dots already ship in all regions [31].

2. **Planning is not execution, and execution is not judgement.** A French journalist who handed her week to ChatGPT found it strong on logistics but blind to context; the piece concludes "L'IA sait ce qu'il faut faire" yet cannot adapt to how the user actually feels that day [104], PRESS, 27/05/2026. Helium's anticipation layer must read the calendar and mail, not just plan them.

3. **Booking agents are unreliable and opaque.** A French travel-industry review of Google Mariner, Accor's ChatGPT app and eDreams' agent cites a finding that 90 % of AI travel plans contain errors, notes prices that differ at checkout, mishandled constraints (reduced mobility, pets, short connections), undisclosed sponsored results and unresolved liability for wrong non-refundable bookings; Accor's agent redirects to its site for payment [105], PRESS, 24/03/2026. A UK test of ChatGPT agent booked a restaurant in two minutes but got "stuck in a loop" on a pizza order [106], PRESS. Confirmation-before-commit (Helium's design) is what these reviews ask for.

4. **Trust is the binding constraint.** 52 % of French trust neither a personal assistant nor a brand AI to act for them; 43 % will delegate only with a final validation step [23]. Distrust drops to 8 % among people willing to share data [23], which argues for progressive permissioning rather than an all-access onboarding.

5. **Human concierges are priced for the top 1 %.** Paris private concierge 3,000-15,000 €/year [78]; family tiers 700-1,000 €/month [79]; corporate concierge reaches under 1 % of employees [74]. The cost barrier is also what blocks household delegation generally (42 % cite cost; 36 % see outside help as a luxury) [22].

6. **Human-powered "butlers" do not scale.** Yohana at $249/month closed in January 2026 after 300,000 tasks [57], [58]; Milo is inactive [64]; Wiidii pivoted to B2B2C [70]. Martin users report 2-4 weeks of onboarding and lagging non-English support [62]: a French-first product with fast onboarding is differentiated.

7. **Family apps are passive and brittle.** Cozi reviews cite sync failures and US-only date formats [108]; Gemini app reviews (NPS −64 in the sample) complain it "doesn't work in Switzerland" and regressed versus Google Assistant [107]. Family coordination is unserved by anything that executes.

8. **Platform choke points.** WhatsApp, the most used messaging channel in France, banned third-party general assistants from its Business API on 15/01/2026 [53]; a Helium WhatsApp front end is therefore not viable, favouring native app, iMessage/SMS and email ingestion.
